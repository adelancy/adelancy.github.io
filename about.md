I'm Adrian Delancy, a software development engineering manager based in Seattle, Washington.

My work sits at the intersection of building software and building the teams that build it. Day to day that means technical direction, architecture, hiring and growing engineers, and the organizational work that turns a group of good people into a group that ships. I still care a great deal about the engineering itself — I don't think you can lead a technical team well from a distance.

## What I write about here

**Software engineering.** Design and architecture decisions, the tradeoffs behind them, and what holds up over time versus what only looked good in the design doc.

**Technical leadership and team management.** Growing engineers, running teams, and the parts of management that are genuinely hard: setting direction under uncertainty, giving feedback that lands, and deciding what not to do.

**Economics.** Incentives, markets, and the way economic thinking quietly explains a lot of what happens inside engineering organizations.

**Mathematics, particularly geometry.** I studied physics and mathematics before I wrote software for a living, and geometry is the part I keep coming back to for its own sake.

**Aviation.** I'm a private pilot currently working on my instrument rating. Flying has turned out to be a good source of writing material — checklists, decision-making under load, and the difference between knowing a procedure and being able to execute it.

## Background

I studied Applied Physics at Purdue University, graduating with honors in 2010 with minors in electrical engineering and mathematics. Before moving into software leadership I spent years in technical consulting, working across cloud architecture, network engineering, and enterprise software delivery — designing systems, then leading the teams that implemented them.

Some earlier work of mine appears in fluid dynamics research on thin-film flows over microtextured surfaces, published in the *Journal of Fluid Mechanics* (2013).
